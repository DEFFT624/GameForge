FRIEND TEST / ABOUT 15–20 MINUTES

Test the teaching.
Not the learner.

Use this page during a call. The learner shares their screen, says what they are thinking, and tries the tasks. The observer writes down confusing moments without giving answers.

Before the call
  • Choose someone with little or no coding experience. Ask whether they have used C# before; do not record their name.
  • Explain: “We are testing the website, not you. Getting stuck helps us improve it. You can stop at any time.”
  • Ask them to open the course in a fresh browser profile or private window for clean progress. Never reset someone’s existing course progress just for this test.
  • Keep this feedback page open. Notes save locally in this browser, not to GameForge or GitHub. Use Copy feedback when done. Do not include private information or recordings.
  • Do not send a localhost link to a friend: it only works on the computer serving the site. Use the hosted link once publishing is ready.
Observer only: reasoning to compare after the learner answers

Task 2: 20 − 5 leaves 15; assignment stores the new value. Task 3: health 1 is greater than zero, so the else branch prints Keep going. Do not reveal these before the learner explains their thinking. Accept correct plain-language explanations; do not require technical vocabulary.

For each task, note whether they completed it independently, needed a hint, or could not finish. A correct guess without an explanation is not evidence of understanding. If they get stuck, record the exact word or step, then help them continue; mark that help in the notes.

Finish with three questions

What felt clearest? Where did you feel lost? What would you change first?

Optional second session: the new smaller lessons

Do this later, after the learner has completed the earlier lessons. Do not jump a complete beginner straight to classes.

  • Functions: after Call a named action and Send a value in, get a result back, ask them to explain calling versus returning. Ask what AddBonus(3) returns and why (observer: 8).
  • Objects: after Give an object its own data, ask what would print if hero.Health were changed to 60 (observer: 60). Then ask how the next lesson keeps hero and rival separate.
  • Game states: after the three small lessons, ask what happens if turns starts at zero (observer: no output), then what switch does with GameOver (observer: default prints Rest).
  • Record the first confusing point in the summary; pause rather than pushing through several misunderstood concepts.
